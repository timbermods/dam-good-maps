### The look

- **D496** (amends D154): **Badwater's colour follows the game.** Partly bad water turns toward badwater by Timberborn 1.1.2.8's own tint, `smoothstep(0.01, 1, c)^0.25`, mixed on display values (the game renders in Gamma colour space, `m_ActiveColorSpace` 0, so its shader maths never goes to linear light; amended 2026-10-29, #678's first reading had it in linear light), so it greys as it turns, as the game shows it: a darker teal-grey near the game's measured mixing zone (`#2E444C`) at a few percent, a grey at a fifth, a dull red-brown at half, crimson when pure; the same tint carries its opacity and its dull surface in both looks (`src/render3d/waterPalette.ts` `WATER_BLEND`, `waterTint`). Both waters darken with depth as the game's do, by its `_DepthGradients` texture (sampled at depth / `_MaxDepth`, 5 levels): the contaminated colour is multiplied by its G ramp, so badwater is lighter on a film and darker deeper (relative to a quarter level, at the cited depths: the texture's raw values as Gamma uses them: 0.80 at half a level, 0.68 at one, 0.46 at two, 0.31 at three; `BADWATER.ramp`, `badwaterBody`), D154's darkening with depth now fitted to the game; clean water's colour is multiplied by its R ramp × 2, which Standard's clean water already follows through its calibration to the game's on-screen colours (D304, D310), where the sky's reflection holds deep water up. Standard's 6 L* rule (badwater under clean water of the same depth) stays, a cap the ramp never meets; badwater's reflection and emission have no depth in them, so a share of its light (`BADWATER.floor`, 0.37 of its display colour, option A's own floor, until an in-game measurement sets it) stays at any depth; the mine pit stays darker than badwater at its calibration depth (#682). D154's "a little steeper so 10–25% bad already reads warm" and its "then warm brown" stop give way to the game's curve; its never-purple rule stays (blue is kept under green once red rises over it, so the middle is a muted warm grey-brown). Read from the game's files, never run: the water material's saved properties (`_MinContaminationThreshold` 0.01, `_MaxContaminationThreshold` 1, `_ContaminationNonlinearity` 0.25, `_Color`, `_Base_Contamination_Color_A`/`_B`, `_Base_Contamination_Intensity_Low`/`_High`) and the D3D11 programs of the shaders `Shader Graphs/PhysicalWaterURP_Opaque` and `Shader Graphs/PhysicalWaterURP_Transparent`, all in `C:\Program Files (x86)\Steam\steamapps\common\Timberborn\Timberborn_Data\resources.assets`; the C# side in `investigation/decompiled/Timberborn.WaterSystemRendering.cs` (contamination uploaded per column as a byte texture, `_WaterOpacity` 1); findings in `investigation/notes/water_and_soil.md` Q8. Ruled by the planning session (Kyler put planning in charge of the order on 2026-10-29), from #678's evidence, 2026-10-29. *Amended 2026-10-30 (the glow):* shallow water partly bad glows as the game's does: its contaminated colour turns toward itself × (3, 1.5, 0) by G^10 × saturate(1 − share / 0.6), G the `_DepthGradients` value at its depth (`BADWATER.glow`, `badwaterGlow`, both looks), so an orange warmth shows on films and the shallowest edges of water partly bad and is gone half a level down and in water three fifths bad or more; it is the "red glow at rock" D334 lists among badwater's cues, in place of High's own red contact at the shore.
- **D135** (with D45, D86, D110, D114, D115, D147, D228, D230, D241, D242, D243, D250, D284, D334, D346, D378; *the falls' mist, spray and splash rings removed: falls as the game draws them, D201 amended 2026-10-29*): The 3D view looks as close to Timberborn in game as it can while every map meaning stays readable; appeal matters as much as readability. Two looks, both switchable by the player and each High effect switchable: **Standard**, the clean look, and **High** (Map look 2: #38's water and soft shadows, #65's lighting and materials, #66's vegetation, phase 3's approved stages, released as `map-look-2-done`), the default on computers that can run it smoothly with an automatic fallback to Standard, and a **Light** look where the browser draws WebGL in software (no multisampling, patterns, shadows or soil blending, models of a few triangles, still water, objects baked into one mesh). Our own art only, never game assets; no image files (patterns are drawn by a shader into a small tiling texture); the 3D chunk stays lazy-loaded. Clean view: slopes drawn as ramps, dead trees as pale bare trunks at their true size, no hazard tape, no arrows, no inflated objects; ground tops coloured by moisture (moist vivid green, dry ground the game's cool, muted grey-brown mauve with grey patches, pulled greyer and less saturated in both looks) (amended 2026-10-29 (planning): follows the game, D496's rule: how much of a top is grass follows the game's desert intensity, SoilMoistureSystem `UpdateDesertIntensity`: none at moisture 0, 1 − (1 − m/5) × 0.7 of it at moisture 1 to 5, all of it above, so green thins toward the edge of the water's reach and through a drought; exact moisture stays the moisture layer's), height as layered bands on walls, a toggle to Height colours; the information layer (Markers, off by default or on by a tool that needs it) holds the slope arrows and the objects enlarged from afar. Our High look adds warm sunlight, ambient occlusion, colour-preserving tone mapping and grade (exposure 1.00), a subtle distance haze, sky, rock strata, soil edges, colour variation, distinct vegetation (pine, birch, oak, blue-berried bushes, white birch trunks, bare dead branches; the shelf icons and ghosts use the same models), the diorama edge, water's finishing touches and visible seasons (in a badtide plants on contaminated ground wither as in a drought, following the same contamination; amended 2026-10-29 (planning): follows the game, D496's rule: D250's seasons are the game's, a held Drought or Badtide day turns the haze to the game's fog colour for it (`Sun.blueprint` `HazardousWeatherFogs`, in both looks, the map's own haze its temperate fog), a dried tile looks like any dry tile, and there is no straw, heat shimmer or sickly sky); no mist, spray or splash rings at falls, as the game draws none (amended 2026-10-29, planning, D496's rule). It follows Timberborn's own references (D334): colour-blind readability of water, badwater and contamination follows the game exactly, with its look and cues (badwater's pink caustics, bubbles and red glow at rock; contamination as orange-red veins, through grass too), accepting the readability the game has with no guaranteed lightness gap and no pattern rule beyond the game's; the tests that checked otherwise are re-based to this decision (D148), none deleted. The map is crisp in both looks with no sharpening or post-process filter: tile boundaries crisp with a narrow transition, anisotropic filtering at least 8× on repeating ground and rock textures, no pass, fog or lighting texture adding blur the game doesn't have. The grass is darker and less yellow toward the game's L* 50–55 in both looks; Standard's cliff faces sit toward the game's lighter blue-grey stone. High's frame rate is measured on dense 256² maps. What the game shows at any quality shows in every look: a held Drought or Badtide day's withered plants are drawn dead in Standard and the light look too (D242's "Standard stays exactly as it is" amended 2026-10-29 (planning): follows the game, D496's rule).

Water moves (D353): the always-on moving water (surface advection, foam threads, wakes and joining seams) goes into both looks, and the optional **Flow** view joins the view bar (cool light on clean water, warm embers on badwater), its paths built in the water worker never synchronously on the main thread and passing the smoothness harness before it merges.
