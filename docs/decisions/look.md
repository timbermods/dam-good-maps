### The look

- **D497** (amends D280 (2) and D135): **Terrain is lit as the game lights it.** Draw it as the game draws it; editing aids get their own channel (layers, marks, outlines), never a changed look. The game renders in Gamma colour space (`globalgamemanagers` PlayerSettings m_ActiveColorSpace 0), so its terrain shader (TerrainURP, read from the game's `resources.assets`) works on display values: a face's colour is its albedo × 0.912 (URP's diffuse share at `_Metallic` 0.05) × (the ambient by its normal + 1.1 × the sun's colour × max(n·L, 0) × its shadow), with a little specular. The ambient is Unity's Trilight (decompiled `Timberborn.SkySystem` `Sun`: `RenderSettings.ambientMode = AmbientMode.Trilight`; the day's colours in `Configurations/Sun.blueprint.json`: sky 0.62/0.63/0.97 from above, equator white from the side, ground 0.28/0.23/0.37 from below) as a light probe, unoccluded; only the sun's shadow (strength 1) darkens a face; `GraphicsQualitySystem` has no ambient-occlusion setting. Its height terms: the ground's altitude colour ramps (`TerrainMaterialMap`: twice the ramp's texel at the ground's height over `AltitudeCeiling` 22; `AltitudeMultiplierGrass_Manual` on grass, `AltitudeMultiplierDesert_Manual` on dry ground, before contamination), and the cliffs' gradient (Cliff, CliffEdge, SlopeCliff: their stone × a tint from (0.416, 0.431, 0.475) at level 0 to (0.941, 0.925, 0.894) at level 22). The probe's in-game shots of roofed terrain show floors under arches and roofs lit flat, as any shade. So both looks light the terrain in display values by that ambient by the normal and the sun by its shadow, with no sky-visibility darkening, no contact shadow and no ambient occlusion; tops take the altitude ramp; a cave's interior, an overhang's and an arch's take the same light as open ground, the sun only where it reaches. D280 (2)'s "a little murky" goes, and D135's ambient occlusion in High. Caves still read while editing through the hollow under the cursor (#528), the Caves layer and the cut, which need no darkness; where something reads less well, the answer is an editing aid in its own channel. Its colours (amended 2026-10-29 (planning): follows the game, D496's rule; #722): the colours the light multiplies are the game's: our ground's and walls' patterns keep their shapes, and their mean albedo, measured in the page with the light off (`tools/albedo-means.ts`), is the game's texture mean (`resources.assets`, the 64² mip, read raw): dry ground the `Desert` texture's (TerrainMaterialMap.blueprint.json `DesertTexture`) and grass the `Grass` texture's, each times its detail texture doubled (`GrassDetailsAlbedo`, mean 140: × 1.098), so 69/57/46 and 74/80/27 of 255; walls the `CliffDetail` texture's, 68/62/52, times the cliffs' gradient, in both looks. The walls' level bands (D135's "height as layered bands on walls", D324's and D334's every other level a shade darker) go with it: the game's cliffs have none, and they were a readability aid baked into the look, which this rule sends to its own channel: Markers' level lines count levels. Its finish (amended 2026-10-29 (planning): follows the game, D496's rule; the game's scene, `level2`'s RenderSettings, and its `Rendering/Volume` profile), in every look, in place of our own (Standard's warm tint, High's tone curve, grade and haze, and their switches): done, the exponential fog (`m_FogMode` 2, density 0.003, as URP's FOG_EXP programs draw it: `exp2(-density / √ln 2 × the view depth)`, toward the day's fog colour or a held hazard's; none looking straight down); done, `m_ReflectionIntensity` 0.65 on the environment's reflection (the game's probe, `sharedassets1.assets` "TimberbornEnvironmentRelfectionProbe", at its roughest mip) with the sun's GGX highlight; done, the post chain (URP's LDR grading under Gamma: +0.15 EV, Neutral tonemapping, contrast +14 round ACEScc's mid grey, saturation +3); not applied, `m_AmbientIntensity` 1.2 (Unity uses it for a skybox's ambient only; the game sets Trilight). Still to follow: whether Unity's Trilight probe is cosine-convolved (the engine's code, not in the game's files; it would lift tops toward the white equator), and bloom (threshold 1.5: highlights only). Planning session, 2026-10-29, on #681 (caves) and #703 (all terrain); the calibration on #722, the finish on its follow-up.
- **D496** (amends D154): **Badwater's colour follows the game.** Partly bad water turns toward badwater by Timberborn 1.1.2.8's own tint, `smoothstep(0.01, 1, c)^0.25`, mixed in linear light, so it greys as it turns, as the game shows it: a darker teal-grey near the game's measured mixing zone (`#2E444C`) at a few percent, a grey at a fifth, a dull red-brown at half, crimson when pure; the same tint carries its opacity and its dull surface in both looks (`src/render3d/waterPalette.ts` `WATER_BLEND`, `waterTint`). Both waters darken with depth as the game's do, by its `_DepthGradients` texture (sampled at depth / `_MaxDepth`, 5 levels): the contaminated colour is multiplied by its G ramp, so badwater is lighter on a film and darker deeper (relative to a quarter level, at the cited depths: 0.61 at half a level, 0.43 at one, 0.19 at two, 0.08 at three; `BADWATER.ramp`, `badwaterBody`), D154's darkening with depth now fitted to the game; clean water's colour is multiplied by its R ramp × 2, which Standard's clean water already follows through its calibration to the game's on-screen colours (D304, D310), where the sky's reflection holds deep water up. Standard's 6 L* rule (badwater under clean water of the same depth) stays, a cap the ramp never meets; badwater's reflection and emission have no depth in them, so a share of its light (`BADWATER.floor`, 0.17 until an in-game measurement sets it) stays at any depth; the mine pit stays darker than badwater at its calibration depth (#682). D154's "a little steeper so 10–25% bad already reads warm" and its "then warm brown" stop give way to the game's curve; its never-purple rule stays (blue is kept under green once red rises over it, so the middle is a muted warm grey-brown). Read from the game's files, never run: the water material's saved properties (`_MinContaminationThreshold` 0.01, `_MaxContaminationThreshold` 1, `_ContaminationNonlinearity` 0.25, `_Color`, `_Base_Contamination_Color_A`/`_B`, `_Base_Contamination_Intensity_Low`/`_High`) and the D3D11 programs of the shaders `Shader Graphs/PhysicalWaterURP_Opaque` and `Shader Graphs/PhysicalWaterURP_Transparent`, all in `C:\Program Files (x86)\Steam\steamapps\common\Timberborn\Timberborn_Data\resources.assets`; the C# side in `investigation/decompiled/Timberborn.WaterSystemRendering.cs` (contamination uploaded per column as a byte texture, `_WaterOpacity` 1); findings in `investigation/notes/water_and_soil.md` Q8. - **D135** (with D45, D86, D110, D114, D115, D147, D228, D230, D241, D242, D243, D250, D284, D334, D346, D378; *the light, High's ambient occlusion, the ground's and walls' colours and the walls' level bands and High's tone mapping, grade and haze amended by D497*): The 3D view looks as close to Timberborn in game as it can while every map meaning stays readable; appeal matters as much as readability. Two looks, both switchable by the player and each High effect switchable: **Standard**, the clean look, and **High** (Map look 2: #38's water and soft shadows, #65's lighting and materials, #66's vegetation, phase 3's approved stages, released as `map-look-2-done`), the default on computers that can run it smoothly with an automatic fallback to Standard, and a **Light** look where the browser draws WebGL in software (no multisampling, patterns, shadows or soil blending, models of a few triangles, still water, objects baked into one mesh). Our own art only, never game assets; no image files (patterns are drawn by a shader into a small tiling texture); the 3D chunk stays lazy-loaded. Clean view: slopes drawn as ramps, dead trees as pale bare trunks at their true size, no hazard tape, no arrows, no inflated objects; ground tops coloured by moisture (moist vivid green, dry ground the game's cool, muted grey-brown mauve with grey patches, pulled greyer and less saturated in both looks), height on walls as the game's cliff gradient, lighter higher up (amended 2026-10-29 (planning): follows the game, D496's rule, D497: the level bands of D324 and D334, every other level a shade darker, were a readability aid baked into the look, so by D497's rule they went to their own channel, Markers' level lines), a toggle to Height colours; the information layer (Markers, off by default or on by a tool that needs it) holds the slope arrows and the objects enlarged from afar. Our High look adds warm sunlight (its tone mapping, grade and haze gave way to the game's fog and post chain in every look, amended 2026-10-29 (planning): follows the game, D496's rule, D497), sky, rock strata, soil edges, colour variation, distinct vegetation (pine, birch, oak, blue-berried bushes, white birch trunks, bare dead branches; the shelf icons and ghosts use the same models), the diorama edge, water's finishing touches and visible seasons (in a badtide plants on contaminated ground wither as in a drought, following the same contamination; amended 2026-10-29 (planning): follows the game, D496's rule: D250's seasons are the game's, a held Drought or Badtide day turns the haze to the game's fog colour for it (`Sun.blueprint` `HazardousWeatherFogs`, in both looks, the map's own haze its temperate fog), a dried tile looks like any dry tile, and there is no straw, heat shimmer or sickly sky); mist and spray at the base of falls and splash rings across the pool below. It follows Timberborn's own references (D334): colour-blind readability of water, badwater and contamination follows the game exactly, with its look and cues (badwater's pink caustics, bubbles and red glow at rock; contamination as orange-red veins, through grass too), accepting the readability the game has with no guaranteed lightness gap and no pattern rule beyond the game's; the tests that checked otherwise are re-based to this decision (D148), none deleted. The map is crisp in both looks with no sharpening or post-process filter: tile boundaries crisp with a narrow transition, anisotropic filtering at least 8× on repeating ground and rock textures, no pass, fog or lighting texture adding blur the game doesn't have. The grass is darker and less yellow toward the game's L* 50–55 in both looks; Standard's cliff faces sit toward the game's lighter blue-grey stone. High's frame rate is measured on dense 256² maps.
- **D154** (with D177, D304, D310, D324; *the mixed water's curve amended by D496; the see-through shallows corrected to the game's measure, #692*): Contaminated ground is a layer: the ground keeps its own look and contamination adds red-orange crack veins, denser and brighter as contamination rises, no solid rust fill, wet and dry contaminated clearly different; a thin outline marks where it ends, drawn only with Markers on.
