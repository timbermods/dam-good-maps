# Kyler's forces-preview feedback (2026-09-28)

Kyler's feedback from testing the forces preview, recorded word for word for Tuesday's session to build.

1. Delete removes what's there, never silently: objects and sources first; if there's only ground, its top block (one level down; under water, the bed's top block), for a single hovered tile or a whole selection, one undo step per press. Select's Delete becomes a menu listing the types present with counts (Everything, Water sources, Badwater sources, Ruins, Trees, Bushes, Slopes and the rest, then "Ground (one level)"), with Ctrl+A for the whole map. The start is never deleted, and Delete sources folds into the menu.
2. Every brush (Raise, Lower, Flatten, Smooth, Naturalize) has three modes in its options: Ground, Water and Both. Ground changes only dry tiles, and never lowers a tile next to water below that water's surface, so rivers and lakes stay where they are and nothing spills. Water changes only wet tiles (the tiles drawn as water), so a bed can be reshaped without touching its banks. Both changes everything, as today. Which tiles are wet is fixed when each stroke starts, so nothing shifts mid-stroke. Brushes always use the map's own water, never a drought or badtide view's. The default is Both; each brush remembers the mode last chosen. In Both, a Lower stroke that starts in water still carves a flowing channel (Smart Lower); no other brush gets an automatic behaviour of its own. Inside an open selection, the mode applies within it. The Wand stays for whole-body jobs (one exact lake or river). Old saved strokes replay exactly (D158).
3. The Badwater view: a notice ("No badwater on this map") on maps without badwater, and a verdict from Kyler, likely removal, once he has compared it with the normal look during a badtide.
4. Waterfalls are too white: falling water keeps its teal colour with white streaks, foam at the landing, gentle spills on one-level steps, and cascades that don't stack into a white wall.
5. No zigzag hatching on badwater, anywhere. Colour-blind readability comes from badwater's lightness, colour, murky surface and bubbles instead.
6. Select, seamless and magical, zero confusion: marking modes as icons; Raise and Lower one level per click (and the Up and Down arrow keys); a level number, starting at the selection's lowest, with Flatten to it, Cut down to it and Fill up to it acting immediately; Dig out folded into Cut down; hovering any action previews its result on the land inside the selection; Esc or X closes; plain one-line tooltips; one undo step each.
7. No force ever leaves a tree leaning. Every tree ends upright on its tile after any force, however hard or repeated, and a tree whose ground can't hold it is removed. Seen after heavy Quake use.
8. Raise "Up to level" and Lower "Down to level," out from behind Precise: always visible, off by default, working for soft and Precise strokes alike, with Ctrl+click on the land to pick the level. Raise never goes above its level and Lower never below; ground already past it is left alone.
9. Four rows, top to bottom: the view bar, unchanged; the tools (Raise, Lower, Flatten, Smooth, Naturalize, Select); the forces (Carve, Craterize, Quake, Erupt, Glaciate); then the active tool's settings and More. The design pass revisits the final look.
10. The land's colours match the game, from Kyler's side-by-side of Near Tara Gorge: dry ground toward the game's pale grey-beige, cliff faces toward its lighter blue-grey stone, and grass a touch lighter. Sampled from his screenshot, matching relationships rather than exact pixels (the game's light depends on the time of day), cheaply, in both looks, alongside the water shades work.
11. Drag from the shelf to place: dragging an icon onto the map places it where released, with its ghost following the cursor (clicking still works); dragging the Start moves the map's one start. And a bug fix: only real files dropped from outside the page can open a file, so dragging an icon never offers to close the map again (it offered to close Near Grand Canyon Colorado and its edits).
12. Edge walls warn, never block a save: on a map being edited, an edge wall becomes a warning in the checks dot with a one-click "Lower the wall" fix (the outer tiles cut down to the land inside, one undo step). Generated maps and Real places still guarantee no walls (D151).
13. One size ring, never two: the force's size ring is drawn once, where the cursor actually is (on the water's surface over water, on the ground elsewhere), in one calm colour, with no leftover rings from Erupt's old preview. Seen as two offset rings, one green and one red, over a lake; a single ring on land is fine.
14. A volcano always looks like a volcano: Erupt's cone stays the dominant shape at every setting, rising to a clear summit (a crater with Summit: Crater), with the flows running down its sides. Heavy flows make it wider and messier, never a shapeless mound, and the surface reads as rock rather than single-tile noise. High Power grows it toward the 22-level ceiling; check that Erupt uses the new ceiling (D244) and doesn't stop at 16. Seen at Power 74, Steep and Heavy flows.
15. Bug: Clear sources leaves ghost sources in the view and the water. After a Flatten or Smooth stroke with Clear sources on, the sources are correctly deleted from the map (Undo after any later edit shows dry ground and no sources), but their discs keep drawing and the water simulation keeps running them, even filling a whole lake that doesn't exist, until another edit forces a refresh. Clearing sources must update the view and the water simulation at once, exactly as deleting a source does, with their water draining away (D260), for every brush with Clear sources.
16. Single-key shortcuts: Z undoes, C redoes, and X closes the selection (as Esc does). Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Y and Ctrl+Shift+Z keep working too. All are ignored while typing in a field. X is Quake's side flip today: move that to a free key; if Z or C already belongs to another shortcut, move that one too; tell Kyler where each went. Update the shortcuts reference and the first-run hints.
17. Carve's river is never too deep unless the player wants it: in Timberborn most rivers are 1–2 deep, which keeps them playable for water crops, building over and crossing; deep rivers overwhelm a map. Carve gets a "River depth" setting in its More panel: a number the player sets freely, from 1 up to the map's height ceiling, or Off; default 2; pinnable like the other details. After the carve, the bed under the river is raised where needed so its water is never deeper than the setting, while the canyon's walls stay as tall as Power makes them. Off leaves the river as deep as the carve makes it. It doesn't apply when Carve leaves a dry canyon. On feature/forces (build, Opus 5.5, high).
18. Carve can leave farmable banks: a "Banks" detail in Carve's More panel sets roughly how many tiles of flat land lie on each side of the river before the canyon's walls, from none (walls down to the water, as today) up to about 10; Auto by default, pinnable like the other details. The width varies naturally, wider on the inside of bends and narrower on the outside, never a constant strip. The banks are flat and level with the river's surface, as along Timberborn's own rivers: the bank's top sits right at the waterline, with the bed below it by the river's depth (item 17), so there's no step between the land and the water's edge, and the banks are moist for crops. Like any low floor, they may flood when the river refills (D307). Glaciate's broad, flat-floored valley is a different effect and stays as it is. On feature/forces (build, Opus 5.5, high).
19. Spring, a new force: it places water sources on a sculpted landscape the way nature would, and never changes the land itself (making channels is Carve's job). A click works over the ring's area (Power: how much water, as the total strength of the sources it adds; Size: the area, up to the whole map; Try another re-rolls which heads get springs; its details in More, on Auto). Water wells up from the ground at the heads, one group after another, and rivers start flowing along the land as it is. It adds to the sources already there; Delete → Water sources then Spring rebuilds a map's water around new terrain. Where it places sources must be accurate and realistic:
   - Placed as Timberborn's own maps place them, as closely as possible: follow the official maps' measurements from the grouped-sources investigation (#78, the rule D313) for how many sources make a group, rows versus clusters, the spacing, which way a row lies across the flow, each source's strength, and where on the land the official maps put them. Never scattered single sources. Where the land allows, a Spring result should be indistinguishable from how the game's own designers place sources. Check it: compare Spring's groups on a few maps with the official maps' figures from #78, and show one result beside an official map in the captures.
   - They stand where water truly begins on this land: valley heads, the feet of cliffs, the tops of drainage lines, each group's strength following how much land drains to it, using the same drainage and source logic as the generator (M9a/M9b), so this water is as natural as a generated map's.
   - Their water must flow downhill to somewhere: off an edge, or into a lake that spills onward. Never into a dead-end hollow where it only floods in place.
   - Rivers, not sheets: Spring prefers heads where the land already channels water (valleys, gullies, cliff feet), so the water forms rivers rather than spreading thinly over flat ground. Where the land has no channels at all, the water spreads as that land makes it.
   - A choice worth playing: among the land's natural heads it picks a mix, some strong springs making big rivers, some weak ones making streams that may dry in a drought, and high ones that fall over cliffs where the land has them, so the water creates choices about damming, pumping and farming.
   Spring is clean water only. It follows the forces' rules (D257, D258): no preview beyond its size ring, the camera never moves, one undo step. It joins the forces' row. Build it on feature/forces (build, Opus 5.5, high), after items 1–18, sharing M9b's drainage and source code rather than copying it.
20. Generate always makes a new map. Today the Seed box keeps its seed, so pressing Generate twice with the same settings rebuilds the identical map (Kyler, generating 128×128 twice: "I see the thumbnail for the second map, but only the first renders"; changing the size gave new land from the same seed). Generate rolls a fresh seed every press. Typing a seed pins it, shown by a small lock beside the box, and Generate then reproduces that map until the player unlocks or clears it; opening a share link pins its seed the same way. The Dice button goes, since Generate is the dice. If a pinned seed's map is shown after a new one was asked for, or the preview and Refine ever show a different map from the card's thumbnail, that's a bug to fix too. Build it on feature/forces (build-light, Sonnet 5, high).
21. Map generation must be fast: "it takes a while to generate some maps; too slow and user interest is lost; map generation performance is important." In M9b (m9b-build, Opus 5.5, xhigh):
   - First measure and report where the time goes at 128² and 256² (land processes, rivers, water settling, checks, each candidate attempt), and how often the first candidate passes, per theme, with why the others fail.
   - The biggest win is fewer failed candidates: fix the commonest reasons candidates fail, so the first attempt passes far more often.
   - Then try the remaining candidates in parallel across the machine's spare cores (capped so several 256² maps don't exhaust a browser's memory), rather than one after another.
   - Show the land as soon as it's shaped and let the water visibly fill in afterwards, so the wait reads as the map coming to life, not a spinner.
   - Set targets with Kyler from the measured numbers (for example, about 2 seconds to the first map at 128² and 5 at 256² on this machine), and make them part of M9b's acceptance.
22. The other versions are visible, not hidden: Kyler sees the thumbnail cycle through candidates and wants to open them. The first map that passes shows at once and stays shown; the other candidates that also pass appear as a small strip of thumbnails as they finish, and a click opens one. The shown map never swaps by itself. The strip's last tile, More, generates further siblings on demand: this strip replaces the separate "Another like this" button (from M9c's fold into M9b), so there is one place for other versions. The generator side is M9b's; the strip itself is drawn in "The page is the editor", so it's built once.
23. One window: Kyler finds the flow of Refine this map, close, then back to settings awful, and wants the generator and the editor in a single interface. That is "The page is the editor" (D232–D234); note his priority on it, to follow the forces' release without delay. Before any of it is built, Kyler holds an extensive question-and-answer round on the UI vision in the planning chat, and the result becomes a written UI brief he approves; nothing of the combined page is built until then. To help that round, prepare a short document of the open design questions and the constraints already known (what the page must hold, the decisions already recorded about it, what the forces sitting and the feedback items changed), in docs/, and tell Kyler when it's ready.
24. Trees within walking reach: show how many trees the start's beavers can actually walk to, along real paths including slopes, within the starting-logs floor's walk (40 tiles), with the logs they hold: for example "Trees within walking reach: 64 (≈ 250 logs)". It reuses the floor's own walking computation. It shows on the map card and in the editor, updating live as the player edits (so a cut-off forest shows at once), as information, not a check: useful even when a map is valid. The number is M9b's; its display goes in "The page is the editor", and in today's editor meanwhile if cheap.
25. Carve's two depths are named apart: the existing Depth detail in Carve's More panel (how deep the cut goes, so how tall the canyon's walls are) is renamed "Canyon depth", and item 17's setting is "River depth" (how deep the water on the canyon's floor may be, default 2). They sit side by side in More. In item 17, the walls are as tall as Canyon depth and Power make them. On feature/forces (build-light, Sonnet 5, high).
26. Dead trees scattered over dry land: on an M9a map in the game, pale dead trees cover nearly all the dry ground, not just near the start, and a pine Kyler clicked on cycle 1, day 1 already said "It died." This is far more than the start's dead-grove fallback (#90: 11 of 240 starts). In M9b (m9b-build, Opus 5.5, xhigh): find where they come from (trees planted on soil the game treats as dry, which the switch to the game's soil rules (D300) addresses, or dead trees placed deliberately as scenery, or both), and report per theme how many dead trees a map has at load and where. Then: living trees only where the game's own soil rules keep them alive; dead trees rare and deliberate (a small drought-killed grove as a feature, or the start's fallback where it's truly needed), never scattered over dry land. Dead trees still yield logs, so this also stops maps giving far more easy early wood than they appear to. Check a sample in the game with the pooled probe batch.
27. A river that starts at the map's edge must flow into the map, not off it: on an M9a map in the game, the row of sources starting a river sat at the edge with the channel open there, so much of its water poured straight off the map. Where a river's head is at the edge, the land beside and behind its sources at the boundary stands at least one level above the water (a natural lip, not a wall along the whole edge), so the water's only way is inward along the river; a river leaves the map only at its other end. In M9b (m9b-build, Opus 5.5, xhigh) with the grouped-sources rule (D314), and checked in the Real places conversion and anywhere a force places sources at an edge. Report how many M9a maps have a river losing water off the map at its head, before and after.
28. Sources look like the game's: in the editor, water and badwater sources are drawn close to how Timberborn draws them (a stone basin with water welling up; badwater's darker and distinct), not as blue discs, so rows of sources read at a glance as they do in the game. It's an original model made in the game's style, never taken from Timberborn's files. A badwater source is drawn at its true 3×3 size. The Markers view keeps their labels (strength and details). In both looks, on feature/high-look (build-light, Sonnet 5, high), with the other look items.
29. Every force is fast, with a choice to watch: Carve sometimes takes 50 seconds to a minute to watch, but sometimes Kyler wants to watch the land change and sometimes he just wants it changed.
   - Fast, the default, is the rule for every force (Carve, Craterize, Quake, Erupt, Glaciate, Erode and Spring): the land is final within about two seconds of the gesture, however long or large the result and whatever the Power. A force's animation is compressed to that time; anything that is only a show (the water filling a new channel, Glaciate's falls starting as the ice retreats, dust, lava's glow) may play on afterwards, visibly but never blocking. The player can act again the moment the land is done.
   - Watch plays each force out slowly and cinematically, about four times as long, so the change can be watched. A new gesture, Esc or a click anywhere jumps the playing force straight to its final land, so Watch never traps the player.
   - The choice is one toggle, Watch, in the view bar beside Sound: off means Fast. It's remembered with the player's other editor preferences, and applies to every force; it has nothing to do with the water's pace (D268).
   - If computing a force is itself slow at high Power or on 256², measure it and make it fast, rather than hiding it behind the animation. Report, for each force, the time from the gesture to the final land in Fast for its largest case at 128² and 256².
   On feature/forces and Glaciate's branch (build, Opus 5.5, high).
