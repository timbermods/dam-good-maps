# collab

Collaborative editing's core (D362, D431, D486; the brief is `docs/COLLAB-BRIEF.md`): one player hosts and keeps the order of operations; each browser rebuilds the map from that one ordered list, so both see the same terrain and water. Headless: it runs in Node and in a worker, and it knows no network, no page and no claims.

**Rules**
- The host alone numbers the log. A request names no author: the channel it came on does. Every request is counted before it is checked, so a refusal never leaves the player's next request out of step. Every refusal is one plain line, the sequence does not advance and the host's map is unchanged (D342).
- A replica applies a commit only if it is the next one: a gap or a duplicate is refused with its reason. After each entry it compares the hash of what the players made directly with the host's; a difference marks the copy `out-of-sync`.
- **Ordering never waits on the water.** The commit carries the operations the host applied, literally, and every peer applies them in the defer mode, at single-player cost. The record and the hash cover only what the players made directly: the ground, the fresh rock, the objects that are not a resource feature's, the trees a force felled (from the operations), the lakes the map keeps. The water, the soil and which trees and bushes stand and live follow from the settled water and are not in them.
- **The water settles in the background**, for the latest sequence only (`water.ts`: `pump(ticks)` is the explicit step; a settle a newer entry overtakes is dropped, since the canonical settle has no memory). When it completes the host announces `settled {seq, since, hash}`: the entries after `since` settled together (the batch a water note names). A replica compares its own settle at that sequence. A checkpoint is taken when a settle completes and one is due.
- **What waits for the settled water**: a force (it plans on the canonical water of the entry before it), a Select action (its start carry), a placement stroke, Remove unfed water, Fill, and a weathering Naturalize stroke. The host refuses these as `busy` until its water has settled for the map as it is (the usual case: the map was quiet while it was aimed); a replica settles first. The author's preview plans on the same basis: `replica.previewForce(gesture)` is busy until the copy's water has settled, then returns the operation's hash. The worker's own preview plans on the live water today (`worker/session.ts` `sessionForceMap`), so it can differ from what is committed; moving it onto this basis is the page piece's change.
- Forces travel as gestures: the page's request with its seed (`ForceGesture`, a Carve's source id and the preview's hash beside it). Each peer computes the result on the agreed map with the same steps the worker takes (`forceStep.ts`); no heights cross the wire. A force whose operation, planned on the host's map, differs from the preview its author saw is refused (the map changed under it); with no preview, any entry since its view that changed the map refuses it. A missing seed is refused, never guessed.
- Whole-map changes (Generate, a real place, another map) are the host's alone. The guests hear `map-opening` first, then the `newMap` entry, then adopt the new map's snapshot.
- **Undo with two players** (`undo.ts`, `records.ts`; brief §5): an `undo` or `redo` request names the author's own action by its record (`{ kind: "undo", target }`, the target being the sequence of the entry that made it). The host decides it like any entry and every peer decides it the same from the same records. Each player keeps the last 50 of their own actions, undone ones included (`RecordLog`).
  - **The rule** (`RecordLog.blocker`): an action's inverse is blocked while an active later action of either player (the same player's too) is built on it, that is, its write mask W meets the later one's touch mask T or their identity keys overlap (INTEGRATION.md's table; a force, a feature operation and a water operation touch the whole map, so they block conservatively). A blocked action stays in the history and becomes undoable when its blocker is undone. An action that left the 50 still blocks what is under it, through permanent fences (per-tile and per-key sequence of the evicted actions that were standing; they travel in the snapshot).
  - **One plain line, naming the blocker**: "ada's Raise in the north-east is on top of this" (`blockLine`; your own is "Your Raise ..."; the place is `placeWords` from the tiles the blocker wrote, north is +y). `undoPlan(author)` and `redoPlan(author)` (on the host and the replica) are what the Undo and Redo keys do: the latest standing action nothing is built on (for Redo, the one taken back last), with the blocked ones it skipped, each with its line.
  - **Redo** is blocked the same way, and also when the ground an action was made on has changed since: an earlier action it was built on was taken back or brought back while it was taken back (`shifted` on its record). Edits are never replayed onto changed land.
  - **Stored values, not a replay.** The action's operations leave the document (or return to it, at their place in its order) and the map that results must equal the action's stored patch put on the map as it stands (`before` values for an undo, `after` for a redo, `MapApplier.history`); a difference is refused ("cannot be taken back without changing ground other edits are on") and nothing changes. So an undo is exactly the stored inverse, whatever the build does. The step is atomic and the claims seam sees it: `EntryCheck` gets a record of the undo's effect (`undoes`: target, redo; its writes are the action's) and a refusal takes it back.
  - **The water** re-settles in the background after an undo as after any edit (it never waits for the water). The seam for the water notes (#406): an undo or redo is an ordinary entry in the settled batch `(since, seq]`, its effect record is `records.get(seq)` (`undoes` set; not an action of its own, never in the 50) and the replica's `applied` event carries it, so the notes can name who changed what ground.
- A rejoin is the host's latest checkpoint (the project the app saves, water settled), the records up to it and the entries since, never the whole history.

**Start from**
- `host.ts` `CollabHost`: `request` (the host's own), `submit(author, request)`, `pumpWater`, `snapshot`, `openMap`. The seam for claims is `EntryCheck`, `(entry, record, state) => string | null`: called AFTER the step is applied and its record made, so `record.writes`, `record.touch` and `record.patch` hold the exact footprint; a string takes the step back (`mark`/`takeBack`) as if it never happened. The default allows everything; `setCheck` replaces it. The claims work (`claims.ts`, `footprint.ts`, `waterNotes.ts`, `claims.schema.json`) plugs in there.
- `replica.ts` `CollabReplica`: `request`, `previewForce`, `pumpWater`, `receive` (commit, reject, settled, map-opening), `adopt`, `fromSnapshot`, and the events a page listens to (`opening`, `newMap`, `applied`, `rejected`, `settled`, `outOfSync`).
- `transaction.ts` `MapApplier`: one entry as one atomic step with its `TransactionRecord` (patch, W, T, keys, and the step's operations with their undo data). `mapState.ts` has the direct state, the literal patch (`difference`, `applyPatch`) and the hash; `touches.ts` has each step's masks (INTEGRATION.md's table: a force, a feature operation or a water operation touches the whole map); `mask.ts`, `records.ts` (each player's last 50, undone state, the blocking rule and the fences of the actions that left), `undo.ts` (the refusal lines, `undoPlan`, `redoPlan`), `place.ts` (a place in words).
- `collab.schema.json` and `validate.ts`: the entry, request, commit, reject, settled, map-opening and snapshot shapes (`messageProblem`, `commandProblem`). An operation inside a command is checked by the session that applies it.
- `forceStep.ts`: `planKeptForce` (plan and run, change nothing), `keepForce`, `applyForce`, `forceBasis`.

**Not here yet**: claims, presence, the transport and the room-code function, the page.
