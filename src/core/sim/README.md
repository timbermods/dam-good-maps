# sim

The game's water and soil rules on a height field: the water model, the exact water simulation, the canonical settle, soil moisture and contamination, drought, and hazard weather.

**Rules**
- **Water must match the game.** The simulation (`rust/water`, run through `water.ts` and `rustWater.ts`) is an exact port of `prototype/watersim.py`, which reproduced the game's own save to 0.001 depth. Keep every operation in the same order; the two agree bit for bit on the golden fixtures. A change to the simulation is a change to `rust/water/src/sim.rs`, then `npx tsx tools/rust/build.ts` (the dev server rebuilds it on save).
- The water written into a file always comes from the canonical settle (`prefill.ts`), never from the editor's warm-started preview (`preview.ts`, PLAN §19.7).
- Water never appears from nowhere: what the pre-fill puts where no source's water goes is taken away once the water has settled (`prefill.ts` `canonicalRun`), and the warm start keeps the pre-fill's water only where something reaches it (D385).
- Every emitter and blocker is handled through its footprint (`model.ts`, PLAN §11.5).
- Changing a rule moves maps: re-pin the golden fixtures (D308).
- A speed-up never moves a byte (D130, D359): `water-speedups` pins every byte of the state, and the bookkeeping the simulation keeps up to date (in Rust) (the active list, wet-neighbour counts, evaporation modifiers) is checked against a rebuild every tick. That bookkeeping follows the water only through `run`: start water through the constructor, never by writing `D` or `C` on a simulation that runs on. Taking water away mid-settle makes a new simulation on the water as it stands (`fed.ts` `withoutUnfed`, the one drain of the canonical settle and the preview); only a finished settle's water is written in place (`prefill.ts` `keepSealed`), and nothing runs it again.

**Start from**: `model.ts` `waterModel`; `prefill.ts` `canonicalSettle`; `water.ts` (the simulation); `soil.ts` `gameSoil` and `soil3d.ts` (the game's own soil rules, on stacked water columns from `columns.ts`: what the build and the validators take); `moisture.ts`, `contamination.ts` (the fast heightfield approximation: the settler's first guess on planned water, the live soil view while the water flows, and Real places' files); `drought.ts` `droughtStorage`; `preview.ts` (editor previews: `PreviewJob`, the one warm-start driver, with its cap per caller); `weather.ts` (Drought and Badtide days, and `HazardRun`, the editor's hazard run step by step); `fed.ts` (which water a source, a stored lake or kept water reaches: the drained tiles of Remove unfed water, and no water from nowhere, D385); `fill.ts` (a Fill's hollow and how long it lasts); `rustWater.ts` (the Rust water's WebAssembly binding) and `waterWasm.ts` (the compiled module, generated by `tools/rust/build.ts`: never edit it); `parallel.ts` (the multi-core water, below). The stacked-column water for terrain above terrain (D448) is in the same Rust crate and module but not wired in: no TypeScript calls it yet (`tools/rust/stack-memory.ts` is its correctness adapter; `rust/README.md`).

**Multi-core water** (`parallel.ts`, #130's design on the Rust water): in a worker that installed a pool (`installParallelWater`: the editor's generator worker, whose helpers, `src/worker/waterStrip.worker.ts`, the page starts in `platform/index.ts` so they are up while the worker is busy), on a cross-origin isolated page (`public/sw.js`) in Chromium and Firefox, a map of 256² and up with enough water runs each `WaterSim.run` on several threads: about 8 at 256², up to 16 at 512², each the same single-threaded Rust module on a strip of rows plus 4 halo rows a side, exchanged through a `SharedArrayBuffer` after every tick (rust/water `Sim::sync_rows` keeps the bookkeeping right, and skips the wet-list rebuild when no halo tile turned wet or dry), the strips balanced by their water. It gives the single thread's bytes by construction (a tile's water after a substep depends only on the water within two tiles of it before; no step adds values across tiles), so the two hand over between runs at any time; everywhere else (Node, WebKit, 128², little water, a seep across strips, a helper failing) it is the single thread. `WaterSim`'s interface is unchanged. Proved by `npx tsx tools/rust/water-identity.ts --threads 4 [--sizes 96,128,256,512]` (Node's worker_threads, every size) and the determinism check's `chromium-threads` and `firefox-threads` engines; a change to the simulation keeps the halo of 4 right (two rows of reach a substep).

**Tests**: `tests/unit/water.test.ts` (against `tests/golden/water.json.gz`, made by `tools/export-fixtures.py`), `water-speedups`, `weather`, `sealedBasins`, `startWater`, `fedFill`; `tests/contract/live-water.test.ts`, `mechanics`, `outflows`, `waterFromNowhere`. Run `npx vitest run tests/unit/water.test.ts`.

**Fluid parity (D337)**: `model.ts` reads emitting tiles and limits from `data/parity.ts`; `fluidTime.ts` re-evaluates countdowns and badtide-only drains for an explicit cycle calendar. Aquifers remain unpowered at map start, including those with a drill. `prefill.ts` caps a seep-fed basin at the anchor's floor plus 0.8 (the forces' copy in `rust/forces` and `prototype/watersim.py` alike). A seep starts disabled and restarts below 0.72, its depth read at the object's own coordinates. Negative emitters (sinks) remove their own kind of water and floor depth at zero: `rust/water/src/sim.rs`, the forces' copy in `rust/forces` and `prototype/watersim.py` implement it alike; rebuild `waterWasm.ts` after changing Rust. `explosion.ts` computes the core sphere, chains and support failures; `doc/blast.ts` settles the resulting view. Water under roofs and the drain's direction limiter remain beyond the heightfield engine.
