# Report

Works: the toggle starts off and survives map, look and riverbed changes. Thin, open, translucent arrows sit 0.025 blocks above wet surfaces, depth-test against terrain and objects, and never write depth. Their unfilled centres and translucent strokes preserve the underlying water detail. Muted grey-green strokes have slightly different opacity/colour in High and Standard. They are stationary, with no extra animation.

Direction and speed come directly from `src/render3d/high/flow.ts`: no new simulation or independently inferred current. Below 0.15 tiles/second the overlay emits nothing; perfectly level water emits zero arrows. Speed raises length from about 1 to 2.65 tiles and reduces spacing from about 6 to 2.8 tiles. A deterministic spatial order avoids frame-to-frame flicker. The whole arrow footprint must remain wet and within 0.08 blocks of the anchor surface, avoiding banks and falls. Zooming close shrinks strokes and length to 30% while retaining world-space spacing.

Verified with the small capture script in Edge: both actual looks render; default-off and toggle-off work; level water emits zero arrows; reversing a test surface slope reverses the estimator's direction; a real riverbed edit changes both settled water and arrow geometry while retaining camera and toggle. No browser errors. Six render states produced exactly three downscaled comparison captures, inspected visually. River overview has 60 arrows, Delta split view 96, river close view 76 (counts are for the entire map, not only the camera crop). No broad test suite or performance benchmark was run.

Limits: this is the existing surface-slope estimate, not authoritative simulated outflow. It inherits ambiguities around source tiles, backwater and discontinuities; it deliberately omits falls. Weak currents and very shallow water are suppressed. Tight bends and narrow banks can lose an arrow; close zoom can admit anchors that did not previously fit. Standard's bright water highlights compete somewhat with the strokes. The product renderer's normal High fallback remains in place. The edit button replays two canonical build snapshots rather than running an interactive settle; production must rebuild from each displayed water update. The demo's single private-scene bridge needs a renderer-owned hook for integration.

Scope: generated original maps and original arrow geometry only. No product source edits, no Timberborn files, no export or session schema changes. This branch starts from high-look `8c975822`; a PR into dev also carries high-look's existing ancestor changes until that work reaches dev. The demo commit itself is confined to this directory.
